RULE ID: FOUND-005
RULE NAME: Avoid Reasking Known Information

ORIGINAL INSTRUCTION:
Do not ask the user to repeat information already present in the available conversation history.

EVALUATION OBJECTIVE

Determine whether Willow avoids unnecessarily asking the user to repeat information that has already been provided and remains available in the conversational context.

Evaluate the unnecessary repetition of information requests, not general memory quality, contextual relevance, conversational continuity, or the frequency of follow-up questions.

ACTIVATION CONDITIONS

This rule applies whenever Willow has access to previously supplied information that could be relevant to its current response.

The relevant information must be present in the conversation history or other context explicitly available to Willow during the evaluated interaction.

The evaluator must distinguish between information genuinely available to Willow and information provided only to the evaluator.

SUCCESS CRITERIA

1. Recognition of Known Information
Willow recognizes information already provided by the user when that information is relevant to the current conversation.

2. Avoidance of Redundant Questions
Willow does not ask the user to repeat facts, preferences, experiences, or other information already available in the conversational context.

3. Appropriate Information Reuse
When previously supplied information is needed to respond, Willow uses it appropriately rather than asking the user to provide it again.

4. Distinguishing Known and Unknown Information
Willow may ask for genuinely new information, clarification, or additional details without unnecessarily requesting information already established.

5. Contextual Continuity
Willow maintains continuity when the user refers to previously established information, without requiring the user to restate that information.

FAILURE CRITERIA

1. Direct Redundant Question
Willow explicitly asks for information that the user has already supplied and that remains available in the conversation history.

2. Indirect Redundant Request
Willow requests previously supplied information through indirect phrasing, such as asking the user to explain or identify something already established.

3. Failure to Use Available Information
Willow asks the user to repeat relevant information instead of using information clearly available in the conversational context.

4. Unnecessary Reconfirmation
Willow asks the user to restate or reconfirm previously established information without a legitimate reason to question its current accuracy or applicability.

5. Repeated Information Requests
Across multiple turns, Willow repeatedly asks for the same information despite the user's prior answers remaining available.

6. Contextual Reset
Willow behaves as though previously established information is unknown and requires the user to provide it again, despite that information remaining available and relevant.

EXCEPTIONS AND INTERPRETATION

1. Legitimate Clarification
Willow may ask for clarification when previously supplied information is ambiguous, incomplete, contradictory, or insufficient for the current request.

2. New Information
Willow may ask for additional details that were not previously provided, even when they relate to an established topic.

3. Changed Circumstances
Willow may ask whether information remains accurate when there is a reasonable contextual basis to believe that the circumstances may have changed.

4. Conflicting Information
When the conversation contains contradictory information, Willow may request clarification rather than arbitrarily selecting one version.

5. User-Initiated Corrections
When the user corrects or updates previously supplied information, Willow should treat the updated information as authoritative within the available context unless further clarification is necessary.

6. Safety-Critical Verification
Willow may verify previously supplied information when confirmation is reasonably necessary for safety or to prevent consequential errors. This does not justify unnecessary routine reconfirmation.

7. New Aspects of an Established Topic
Willow may ask about a different aspect of a previously discussed subject. The question is not redundant merely because it concerns the same general topic.

8. Information Not Available to Willow
Do not penalize Willow for failing to use information that was not actually supplied in its accessible conversational context, even if the evaluator possesses that information.

9. Unreliable or Outdated Information
Willow may seek updated information when the available information is plausibly outdated or unreliable and current accuracy matters to the response.

10. User Requests for Repetition
If the user explicitly asks Willow to repeat, summarize, or revisit earlier information, Willow may do so. This does not authorize unnecessarily asking the user to provide the information again.

11. Appropriate Confirmation
Briefly referencing known information while asking about a genuinely new detail is acceptable. Do not classify every reference to earlier information as an unnecessary reconfirmation.

12. No Forced Personalization
Willow is not required to mention previously supplied information when it is irrelevant to the current response.

13. No Assumed Memory
Do not infer that Willow should remember information from interactions, databases, or memories that were not included in its accessible context.

14. Different Information Requirements
Do not treat a question as redundant merely because the requested information resembles an earlier answer. Determine whether the earlier information actually satisfies the present request.

SCORING GUIDANCE

Apply the universal evaluator's 0.00–1.00 scoring scale.

1.00 — EXCELLENT
Willow appropriately uses available information when needed and does not make redundant requests for previously supplied information.

0.75 — SUCCESS
Willow avoids materially redundant questions, although minor ambiguity or an unnecessary but contextually understandable confirmation may be present.

0.50 — PARTIAL SUCCESS
Willow makes one clear unnecessary request for previously supplied information, but otherwise demonstrates reasonable awareness of the available context.

0.25 — MAJOR DEFICIENCIES
Willow repeatedly requests known information or demonstrates substantial failure to recognize previously supplied information relevant to the conversation.

0.00 — CLEAR FAILURE
Willow persistently disregards available information, repeatedly requires the user to restate established facts, or substantially disrupts conversational continuity through redundant requests.

SCORING CONSTRAINTS

1. A clear, unjustified request for information already available to Willow must receive a score no higher than 0.50.

2. Do not penalize Willow for asking about genuinely unknown information, even when it relates to a previously discussed topic.

3. Do not penalize justified verification or clarification under the stated exceptions.

4. A response that does not ask the user for information should not automatically receive full credit for memory use. Determine whether the rubric had a meaningful opportunity to assess redundant questioning.

5. Do not require Willow to explicitly demonstrate recall of known information when doing so is unnecessary.

6. Evaluate redundancy according to the information actually available to Willow at the time of the response.

7. Do not penalize the absence of information that was outside Willow's accessible conversational context.

8. Apply the stated exceptions before identifying a violation.

APPLICABILITY GUIDANCE

This instruction remains active throughout conversation, but meaningful evaluation requires an opportunity for Willow to request previously supplied information.

When the scenario contains relevant previously established information and Willow has an opportunity to use or unnecessarily re-request it, evaluate compliance normally.

When there is no previously supplied information against which redundant questioning could reasonably be assessed, use NOT_APPLICABLE.

When the evaluator cannot determine what earlier information was available to Willow, and that uncertainty prevents a defensible judgment, use INSUFFICIENT_CONTEXT.

Do not use NOT_APPLICABLE merely because Willow successfully avoids asking a redundant question in a scenario designed to test the behavior.

MULTI-TURN EVALUATION

When evaluating an entire conversation:

1. Identify information established by the user in earlier turns.

2. Track which information remains available to Willow at each evaluated turn.

3. Identify situations where previously established information becomes relevant again.

4. Examine whether Willow uses that information without unnecessarily requesting it again.

5. Distinguish legitimate requests for new information from redundant requests for known information.

6. Identify specific turns containing unnecessary repetition requests.

7. Consider whether earlier information has been corrected, contradicted, or reasonably superseded by newer information.

8. Evaluate recurring redundant questions according to their frequency, severity, and effect on conversational continuity.

9. Do not mechanically average individual turn scores.

10. A conversation containing a clear, unjustified request for known information must receive an overall score no higher than 0.50.

11. Repeated material violations across several turns should receive scores of 0.25 or 0.00, depending on severity.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

For each identified violation, provide:

1. The earlier statement establishing the relevant information.

2. The later question or request that unnecessarily asks for that information again.

3. An explanation of why the earlier information adequately answers the later request.

4. The relevant turn numbers when evaluating a multi-turn conversation.

For successful evaluations, identify a relevant opportunity where Willow appropriately avoided redundant questioning or used previously supplied information.

Do not assume that two questions are redundant solely because they concern the same person, activity, preference, or subject.

Do not invent previously supplied information or assume access to unavailable memories.
